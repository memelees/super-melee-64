Melee Mario / SM64 Mario — local source and runtime comparison
24 October 2026

Scale and clock
The bridge maps one native Melee distance unit to ten SM64 units. Melee runs
at 60 ticks/second; SM64 runs at 30 world frames/second. A Melee velocity of
1 therefore corresponds to 20 SM64 units per world frame. A native gravity
of 0.1 corresponds to 4 SM64 units per world frame squared. The speed option
changes attributes, never either engine's simulation clock.

Visual calibration checked 3 November 2026: actual skinned standing Melee Mario
is 148.35 SM64 units tall. Stock Mario is 149.94 in center idle, varying from
144.03 to 149.98 across 90 idle frames; castle star doors are 256 units high.
Melee Mario therefore already matches the stock visual scale within ordinary
pose variation. Melee Link measures 184.85 units (about 25% taller than Melee
Mario), retaining the native roster proportions. These are visual mesh bounds,
not Mario's 160-unit collision height. See verification/scale-calibration.json.

Expressed in SM64 units per 30-Hz world frame:
- Original Melee Mario walk/run limits: 22 / 30.
- Default boosted Melee Mario walk/run limits: 27.5 / 37.5.
- SM64 normal ground target: 32; slow surfaces use 24. SM64 also permits
  overspeed up to 48 in its walking update, so 48 is not the normal run target.
- Melee Mario full-jump launch: 46. SM64 normal jump launch: 42 plus 0.25 times
  forward speed, or about 50 at its normal 32 run target.
- Melee Mario gravity: 3.8 versus SM64 ordinary gravity 4.
- Melee Mario ordinary fall limit: 34 versus SM64 ordinary fall limit 75.
These are limits/initial attributes, not a claim that every input instantly
reaches them. Analog input, acceleration, slopes and state transitions matter.

State comparison and integration choices
SM64 idle/walking: Melee Wait, WalkSlow/Middle/Fast, Dash and Run. Melee retains
its separate dash startup and running state; SM64 instead changes gait within
its walking action. A partial stick generally walks rather than starting a dash.
The host now maps SM64's already-processed radius-64 stick to Melee radius1.
The earlier divisor80 limited full input to0.8 and could suppress dashing.
Normal keyboard play verified Dash(20), Run(21), RunBrake(23), and an actual
boosted Mario run speed of 37.5 SM64 units/frame, matching the attribute limit.

SM64 braking/turning: Melee Turn, TurnRun and RunBrake retain native timing;
Melee3D supplies the world-space heading/collision lane. This is not identical
to SM64's curved momentum and slope acceleration.

SM64 jump/double/triple jump: Melee KneeBend, JumpF/B and JumpAerialF/B. SM64's
double/triple jumps are timed ground-jump chains (launch 52 + momentum, then69).
Melee's second jump happens in the air. Characters retain their own jump count,
short hops, recovery moves and startup; no synthetic SM64 triple jump is added.

SM64 falling/landing: native Melee Fall and Landing families, including aerial
attack landing lag and helpless FallSpecial after applicable recoveries.
Melee Mario falls more slowly than SM64 Mario; increasing horizontal speed
alone does not change that difference.

SM64 ledge hanging/climbing: native Melee CliffCatch/CliffWait, climbs, rolls,
attacks and ledge jumps using live SM64 geometry and platform carry.

SM64 wall kicks: native Melee wall jumps for fighters whose native ability
allows them. A shared movement boost does not grant wall jumps to every fighter.

SM64 doors, painting transitions, cannons and scripted actions: SM64 keeps its
normal world/script handling. Native simulation pauses for those handoffs.

Why start at 1.25x
This raises Mario's walking traversal toward SM64's normal target while allowing
his separate native run to be a little faster. It preserves each character's
relative speed. It is a configurable playtest choice, not an exact movement
match or a finding that all characters need the same final tuning.

The existing MELEE3D_NATIVE_MOVE_SPEED setting scales native horizontal movement
attributes (including acceleration, friction, air drift and ledge/wall-jump
horizontal launch). It is applied when attributes rebuild from original DAT
values, so respawns and transformations do not compound the multiplier.
Attack animation clocks, native vertical jump velocities, gravity, weight and
the initial damage/knockback formula are not sped up. Character-specific special
move/root-motion attributes are not all covered by this common speed modifier.
Use native/play_embedded.py --speed 1 to restore the original movement tuning.
